# Operating Systems question-bank audit

## Outcome

All **279 original items** were individually reviewed against the cited material and their intended answer was independently worked through. **279 verified, 172 corrected, 107 unchanged, and 0 held or excluded.** Of the verified items, 56 are directly supported by the stated slides, 209 apply a rule or model shown in those slides to a scenario, and 14 rely on a supplementary technical source for a material detail. No item relies on a published StudyMAXIN lesson from this course. All 279 IDs and correct-answer index positions are unchanged.

The [machine-readable audit record](quiz-audit.json) has one entry per original ID. It records disposition, subtopic, reviewed answer index, physical slide positions, the evidence-specific rationale and any corrections. It explicitly separates directly supported items, applications of slide rules, supplementary references and unresolved evidence. For each item, `visualSlides` lists only the cited positions where one or more relevant slide images were actually inspected. A blank list means the item was checked using readable extraction and context, with no claim of image inspection for that item.

## Source method and corrections

The PDFs in ignored `materials/` cover Information Systems Security. The five original Operating Systems PPTX decks were located privately in Downloads and independently SHA-256 matched against the course manifest. The PPTX presentation relationships determine one-based slide positions. A physical slide can contain several embedded slide images, and a printed page or figure number may differ from that position. The review used extraction from those exact deck images and inspected the images where formulae, diagrams, code, or OCR uncertainty mattered. The ledger records visual corroboration for 153 items. It does not equate an image check of a relevant diagram with a visual check of every slide cited by that item. No source files, slide images, or OCR dumps enter the static export.

For each item, the review checked its natural reading and technical model, all four choices, the independently derived key, explanation, level, weekly scope, and cited positions. Source references were changed for 23 items. Representative fixes include specifying the no-conflict cache trace, defining FIFO arrival order, distinguishing short-term selection from dispatcher handoff, bounding the process memory layout to the course diagram, and clarifying that reader/writer scenarios use an abstract interleaving model.

The review found several lecture simplifications that could otherwise propagate to the trainer:

- Week 3 slide 47 illustrates a pair of endpoints, but its broad claim about different ports should not be used to infer that every accepted connection needs its own server port. TCP byte stream boundaries and the endpoint tuple were independently confirmed. [RFC 9293 sections 3.4.1 and 3.7](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9293.html#section-3.4.1), [Linux accept documentation](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/accept.2.html#DESCRIPTION).
- Week 4 slide 37 describes `exec` as duplication. The corrected question uses program-image replacement and explains why copies of the previous program's threads would be unnecessary. The Java `run` question explicitly identifies the platform-thread construction shown in the lecture. [Linux execve documentation](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/execve.2.html#DESCRIPTION), [Java 21 Thread API](<https://docs.oracle.com/en/java/javase/21/docs/api/java.base/java/lang/Thread.html#run()>).
- Week 5 slide 15's prose is less precise than its Peterson loop. Its guard is a conjunction of the other thread's flag and the turn test, so the audit used the code for the outcomes and stated the ordering model. The blocking semaphore diagram at slide 27 has ambiguous brace placement around `block`; the revised count exercise states explicitly that only a negative result blocks. The monitor review distinguishes reacquiring the monitor and testing the predicate again from receiving a semaphore permit. [Mesa language manual](https://bwlampson.site/23a-MesaManual/23a-MesaManualOCR.htm), [POSIX condition signal description](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man3/pthread_cond_broadcast.3p.html#DESCRIPTION).

Supplementary questions display the slide as topic context and expose the separate verification links in the existing source disclosure. Direct questions and slide applications retain their ordinary lecture citations. Historical attempt counts and grades keep the same correct-answer mapping. Past results look up the current wording of an option, so an edited distractor can display its clarified wording in a historical result. No ID replacements or migrations were needed.

## Duplication and difficulty findings

There were zero normalized exact stem duplicates. Review concentrated on near-overlap within and across weeks. The loader pair now distinguishes identifying a loading utility from its role after shell command dispatch. The Week 1 mode question asks whether a mode change implies a process switch, while the Week 3 question classifies a concrete executing process by mode and scheduling state. Week 3's workload-mix scenario now exercises the ready-queue consequence of adding CPU-heavy work rather than repeating generic multiprogramming. The remaining SJF, Amdahl, Peterson, and semaphore families are deliberately retained. Their variants exercise different inputs, an inverse calculation, relative ordering, or differences between mechanisms. Some are still strongly correlated because the mock samples questions independently from the full bank.

As a wording check, the correct choice was the sole longest by whitespace-separated word count in 56 items before editing and 33 after editing. The number of items with a correct choice of at least six words and at least 50% longer than every distractor fell from four to two. Choice length is a wording diagnostic. Learner responses would be needed to estimate how easily students can guess an answer.

Eight difficulty labels were recalibrated. Direct symbol recall and straightforward protocol ordering moved downward. Multi-step interleavings, dependency reasoning, and comparison of incomplete invariants retain advanced labels. Difficulty is an editorial classification. No learner-response data were available to estimate difficulty or distractor discrimination.

## Deployment recommendation

The corrected bank is ready for a self-study release. On Node 24.22.0, `npm run check` passed all 47 unit tests and built 419 static pages. All 26 Chromium tests passed against that export. It has more than the 60 verified items required for each mock, with question and option shuffling preserved. Give borderline lecture phrasing and heavily represented question families priority in a future educator review. Results in the trainer provide formative feedback. There has been no independent instructor sign-off, learner pilot, or validation of its use as a graded university exam.

The server checks that every authored ID has exactly one record, that its reviewed key and slide citation agree with the authored record, and that supplementary items carry HTTPS verification links. Questions marked `requires-review` stay out of the published bank and therefore all session modes. A build fails with fewer than 60 verified items. These integrity checks reinforce the recorded review decisions. They do not perform factual review on new or edited content.
